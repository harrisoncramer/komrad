## The different views

The panel is divided into a few regions.

The sidebar holds a list of whatever is in focus: agents and workflows, schedules, worktrees, or documents. Under the list are the details, which show what is known about the row under the cursor, such as an agent's directory or its base branch. Under those, the messages region shows what komrad has recently reported, and the legend shows the keys that work from where you are.

The switcher across the top of the sidebar moves between the agents, the query, the schedules, the archive and the worktrees. Press <Key name="views.pick" /> to reach it, then the underlined letter of the view you want.

The stage is the wide region beside the sidebar. It shows the agent you have opened, its diff on the review screen, or a document.

<Key name="cycle.views" at={0} /> moves the keyboard to the next of these regions, and <Key name="cycle.views" at={1} />
to the previous one: the list, the agent on the stage, then the details. `ctrl+g` moves it to the messages. If your own
tmux prefix is <Key name="cycle.views" at={0} />, choose another
<Setting path="keybindings.cycle.views">pair of cycle keys</Setting>.

## Basic navigation

Every list moves the same way. <Key name="nav.down" /> and <Key name="nav.up" /> move the cursor a row, <Key name="nav.page_down" /> and <Key name="nav.page_up" /> move it a chunk of rows, and `gg` and <Key name="nav.bottom" /> jump to the first and last row. The mouse wheel scrolls whichever region it is over.

Press `/` to search. Typing lights up the rows that match, and <Key name="agents.open" /> puts the cursor on the nearest one. <Key name="filter.next_match" /> and <Key name="filter.prev_match" /> then step through the matches. A pattern in lower case ignores case, and a pattern is a regular expression.

While typing a search, press `tab` to turn it into a filter, which takes every row that does not match off the screen instead of stepping through them. <Key name="agents.back" /> clears either one and puts the cursor back where it started.

Press <Key name="tabs.focus" /> to reach the sort strip over the list, where you pick the column to sort by. Its last stop groups the rows under their workflows, and flattening them makes the list easier to sort by activity or name.

You can also mark a row with <Key name="marks.set" /> and a letter, and jump back to it with <Key name="marks.jump" /> and the same letter, the way vim's marks work.

## Subpages

Some screens belong to one row rather than to a whole view. An agent has the review of its work, the documents it has written, and its logs. Press `space` on an agent, in the agents list or the archive, to open a menu of its subpages. On a workflow's row the menu opens on its orchestrator. Each entry answers the letter underlined in its name, or `j`, `k` and `enter`, and `esc` closes the menu. Each of these keys can be [rebound](../../config/#keybindings.subpages). An entry with nothing behind it is gray, and pressing its letter says why in the messages. `esc` on the subpage you picked goes back to the list you opened the menu from.

The git menu on `b` works the same way, with an entry to rebase the agent's branch onto its base and one to open its pull request.

## Visual selection

Press <Key name="selection.toggle" /> to take the row under the cursor into a selection, and <Key name="selection.range" /> to take in every row between the cursor and the nearest selected one. An action that works on one agent then works on all of them, such as archiving, or restoring and deleting on the archive screen.

## The details

The details under the list show more about the row under the cursor, such as an agent's directory, branch, pull request and recent commits. Press <Key name="cycle.views" at={0} /> until the keyboard reaches them, and press <Key name="details.copy" /> to copy the value of the field under the cursor to your clipboard. `/` searches the fields the same way it searches the list.

Above the fields, each mark the row draws in the sidebar is said in words, such as unread mail, a failed script, or where its pull request stands: failing, conflicting, approved, or still a draft.
